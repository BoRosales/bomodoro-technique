About the Pomodoro Technique

The Pomodoro Technique is a time-management method created by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s. It's named after the tomato-shaped kitchen timer he used as a student (pomodoro is Italian for "tomato").

The idea is simple: work in short, focused bursts with regular breaks in between. Short sessions make big tasks easier to start, help you resist distractions, and the breaks keep you fresh, so you can stay productive for longer without burning out.

How to use Bomodoro

  1. Pick one task to work on and add it to your task list. Keep it specific, like "draft the intro" instead of "work on essay".
  2. Press Start to begin a focus session (currently {{ settings().focusMinutes }} minutes).
  3. Stay on that task until the chime plays. If a distraction pops up, jot it down and come back to it later.
  4. Take a break. When the chime plays, the timer switches to your break ({{ settings().breakMinutes }} minutes). Press Start, then step away: stretch, grab water, rest your eyes.
  5. Repeat. When the break ends, the timer is ready for your next focus session.

Tips

  • The classic method uses 25-minute focus sessions with 5-minute breaks, plus a longer 15–30 minute break after every four sessions. Keep an eye on your session count and treat yourself to that longer rest.
  • Not everyone works best in 25-minute blocks. Try different lengths in Settings to find your rhythm.
  • Need to step away mid-session? Press Pause; your progress is saved, even if you close the tab.
